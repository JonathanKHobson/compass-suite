01 / Outcomes

Work at the level the draft needs.

The collection separates architecture, scene craft, publishing, learning, and play so each pass has a clear question.

  1. 01Find the spine

    Clarify the promise, pressure, turns, and ending before polishing paragraphs.

  2. 02Direct the scene

    Use desire, obstruction, consequence, pacing, and concrete detail to make a scene move.

  3. 03Prepare for people

    Turn the material into a teachable, publishable, or playable experience with limits and rights visible.

02 / Practice

Several doors into the same project.

Use the specialist that matches the current job, then bring its evidence into the next pass.

A

Story architecture

Shape premise, character pressure, sequence, escalation, and payoff.

B

Scene and line craft

Revise pacing, dialogue, description, and show-versus-tell choices in context.

C

Publishing and provenance

Check originality, rights, attribution, format, and the right publishing route.

D

Learning and live play

Adapt the material for teaching, role-play, facilitation, or a table in motion.

03 / Example

Ask for a decision, not “better writing.”

A bounded prompt makes the requested change and its evidence inspectable.

Starting prompt

Use story architecture, then pacing-scene-director, on this chapter.\n\nName the scene’s active want, obstacle, and turn. Show me the one beat carrying no pressure. Propose two repairs without rewriting in a different voice.

Useful return

A short scene diagnosis, the evidence in the draft, two distinct repair options, and the tradeoff each creates for the next scene.

04 / Fit

Use it for the work it can actually support.

Good fit

Stories, scripts, learning scenarios, role-play, campaign preparation, publishing choices, and revisions that must preserve a real voice.

Not a substitute for

Your authorship, rights review, sensitivity expertise, subject-matter research, or the judgment of the people who will read or play the work.
