01 / Outcomes

Prepare what helps at the table.

The collection keeps prep responsive: strong pressure, legible stakes, and several ways the players can change the situation.

  1. 01Build playable prep

    Prepare people, places, pressures, clues, and consequences that can survive an unexpected choice.

  2. 02Direct the moment

    Manage pacing, spotlight, NPC intent, scene turns, and failures that redirect rather than stall.

  3. 03Support the room

    Teach rules in context, invite player contribution, use safety tools, and adapt access without singling people out.

02 / Practice

Before, during, and after play.

Use the specialist that matches the current job, then bring its evidence into the next pass.

A

Story atlas prep

Organize campaign truth, open questions, factions, locations, player hooks, and likely pressure points.

B

Live table direction

Read the room, share spotlight, frame choices, redirect failure, and end on a changed state.

C

Player facilitation

Invite authorship without forcing performance and turn uncertainty into an approachable choice.

D

Safety and access

Set expectations, support boundaries, teach tools plainly, and adjust the experience with dignity.

03 / Example

Prepare branches, not outcomes.

A bounded prompt makes the requested change and its evidence inspectable.

Starting prompt

Use story-atlas-prep and live-table-director for tomorrow’s two-hour session.\n\nPreserve the canon below. Prepare one opening pressure, three clues that can move, two NPC intentions, and consequences for delay. Do not decide what the players choose.

Useful return

A compact table sheet with a start state, movable evidence, active NPCs, consequences, and clear places to hand authorship back to the players.

04 / Fit

Use it for the work it can actually support.

Good fit

Session prep, campaign continuity, scene pressure, NPC performance, new-player teaching, public games, safety planning, and post-session debriefs.

Not a substitute for

Consent, the rules text, table knowledge, cultural expertise, professional care, or the judgment to pause when a real person needs attention.
